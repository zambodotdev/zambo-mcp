## The 7 most common gaps (with real numbers)

**1. Nobody is in the AI directories — 9 of 9.**
Every single site was flagged for AI-directory verification/submission (Futurepedia, There's An AI For That, Toolify, AiTopTools, Product Hunt). Even Cursor, the 82/100 outlier, got this as one of its two HIGH findings. The entire web's AI-era Yellow Pages are effectively empty of their biggest names.

**2. agent.json is nearly nonexistent — 7 of 9 missing.**
Only Cursor and Vercel ship a machine-readable agent manifest. Figma, Stripe, Notion, Shopify, Netlify, Raycast, Perplexity: nothing. Note the path disagreement is already a problem — Vercel serves it at `/agent.json`, Cursor at `/.well-known/agent.json`. Agents have to guess.

**3. llms.txt: present but half-baked — 7 of 9 have a gap.**
3 sites have no llms.txt at all (Figma, Raycast, Perplexity — all 404, verified). Of the 6 that do, 4 were flagged "needs improvement": missing MCP config examples, missing contact info, missing pricing or tool catalogs. A 200 status with an empty file is a checkbox, not a strategy.

**4. No comparison pages — 8 of 9.**
Only Cursor has /vs/ or /compare/ pages. Everywhere else, the highest-intent search traffic on earth ("X vs Y", "best X alternative") is being captured by third-party affiliates and competitors. This is an SEO finding with AI-era teeth: agents asked "what's better, X or Y?" currently learn the answer from someone else's site.

**5. Schema.org structured data missing — 4 of 9.**
Figma, Notion, Raycast, and Perplexity ship no structured data. Stripe, Netlify, and Shopify do. Machines read schema; without it, your pricing, features, and product identity are vibes.

**6. Sitemaps missing at giants — 2 of 9.**
Stripe and Netlify have no sitemap.xml the crawler could find. Crawlers can still work without one, but you're making every bot (search and AI alike) do extra work to discover your pages.

**7. "Free tier not visible" — 7 of 9 flagged.**
Take with salt (see caveats): JS-heavy homepages render thin to automated fetchers, so some of these are false negatives. But the underlying point stands — if a bot can't see your free tier, neither can the agent deciding what to recommend.

## Caveats — read before quoting

1. **Perplexity.ai: excluded from rankings.** The audit crawler hit Cloudflare's "Just a moment..." bot challenge and audited the challenge page (16-character title, no meta, no content). The 0/100 and 48 findings describe that page. The irony is itself a finding: the web's bot-protection layer and its AI-discoverability layer are currently at war, and an AI company is collateral damage.
2. **Notion's 0/100 is partly a crawler artifact.** Notion's homepage is a JS-heavy app shell; the fetcher saw a thin page and reported false "no CTA", "no privacy policy", "no free tier visible" findings — all of which exist on the real site. But that *is* the AI-readiness problem in miniature: if your site doesn't server-render meaning, machines (search crawlers and AI agents alike) see a worse version of you than humans do.
3. **linear.app: blocked, not scored.** The 10th audit hit the free-tier quota wall (`payment_required`, $1.49 USDC Day Pass) after 9 audits. No payment was made. It would be dishonest to score it, so it isn't.
4. **The grader grades on its own rubric.** Achilles weights AI discoverability heavily and cites Zambo's own llms.txt as the gold standard. Treat scores as directional — the underlying HTTP facts (the matrix above) are the hard data.
